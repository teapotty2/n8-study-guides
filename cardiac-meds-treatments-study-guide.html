Cardiac Medications & Treatments

A focused study guide covering drug classes, dosing, nursing considerations, and exam-critical details from N8 cardiac lecture content

1. Hemodynamic Drugs HEAVY IN SLIDES

A B C D ACE/ARBs • Beta-blockers • Calcium Channel Blockers • Diuretics

1.1 Preload Reducers

DrugMechanismKey Nursing Points
Nitroglycerin (NTG)Venodilation → ↓ preload → ↓ myocardial O2 demandHold if SBP <90; 3-dose SL protocol
NitroprussideArterial + venous dilation (both preload and afterload)Cyanide toxicity risk; wrap IV bag in foil (light-sensitive)

1.2 Afterload Reducers

ClassExamplesKey Points
ACE Inhibitors (-pril)Lisinopril, enalapril, captoprilDry cough, angioedema risk, monitor K+ (hyperkalemia)
ARBs (-sartan)Losartan, valsartanSame benefits as ACE, no cough; use if ACE-intolerant

1.3 Beta-Blockers (-olol)

Metoprolol, atenolol, carvedilol, labetalol. Decrease HR, contractility, and BP.

Hold parameters: HR <60 bpm or SBP <100 mmHg. Always check HR and BP before administering.

1.4 Calcium Channel Blockers

TypeExamplesPrimary Effect
Dihydropyridine (DHP)Amlodipine, nifedipinePrimarily BP reduction (vasodilation)
Non-DHPDiltiazem, verapamilBP reduction + rate control (slow AV node)
Non-DHP = Node-blockers. Diltiazem and verapamil also slow the heart — they're used for A-fib rate control.

1.5 Vasopressors

DrugMechanismUse
Norepinephrine (Levophed)Alpha-1 + some beta-1 → vasoconstriction + modest inotropyFirst-line for septic shock; central line only
VasopressinV1 receptor → vasoconstriction (non-catecholamine)Adjunct in cardiac arrest and septic shock
PhenylephrinePure alpha-1 agonist → ↑ SVRHypotension without tachycardia; may cause reflex bradycardia
"Levophed — Leave 'em dead." Potent vasopressor — handle with care, always via central line, monitor closely.
Preload = volume coming IN (NTG reduces it). Afterload = resistance going OUT (nitroprusside, ACE, ARBs reduce it).

2. Inotropes & Chronotropes HEAVY IN SLIDES

2.1 Key Definitions

TermMeaningPositive ExampleNegative Example
InotropyContractilityDigoxin, dobutamineBeta-blockers, non-DHP CCBs
ChronotropyHeart rateAtropine, epinephrineBeta-blockers, diltiazem
DromotropyConduction speedAtropineAdenosine, amiodarone
"Chrono = time = rate. Dromo = running = conduction speed."

2.2 Positive Inotropes

DrugMechanismClinical UseKey Nursing Points
DigoxinInhibits Na/K-ATPase → ↑ intracellular Ca → ↑ contractilityHF, A-fib rate controlTherapeutic: 0.5-2.0 ng/mL; hold if HR <60; check K+
DobutamineBeta-1 agonist → ↑ contractilityAcute decompensated HFMay cause tachycardia; monitor HR
MilrinonePDE-3 inhibitor ("inodilator")Acute HF↑ contractility AND vasodilation
DopamineDose-dependent: renal → cardiac → vasopressorMultiple usesLow: renal; Moderate: cardiac; High: vasopressor
Dopamine doses: "Dope Low = kidneys flow" (1-5 mcg/kg/min), Moderate = cardiac pump (5-10), High = squeeze vessels (10-20).
Digoxin toxicity signs: N/V, anorexia, visual disturbances (yellow-green halos), bradycardia, heart block. Hypokalemia increases toxicity — always check K+ level. Antidote: Digoxin immune Fab (DigiFab).

4. Nitroglycerin — Deep Dive HEAVY IN SLIDES

NTG = Needs To Go to veins Primarily a venodilator → decreases preload → decreases myocardial O2 demand

4.1 Mechanism

Releases nitric oxide → relaxes vascular smooth muscle → venodilation (primarily) → decreased preload → decreased myocardial oxygen demand. Think: "Decreases the heart's workload."

4.2 Sublingual Protocol

  1. 1 tablet sublingual — wait 5 minutes
  2. If no relief: 2nd tablet — wait 5 minutes
  3. If no relief: 3rd tablet — call 911 / activate emergency response

Patient should sit or lie down before taking (hypotension risk).

4.3 Routes & Administration

RouteKey Points
Sublingual tabletsShould tingle/burn under tongue if potent
Sublingual sprayNo storage concerns like tablets
IV dripFor unstable angina, acute HF, HTN emergency. Use glass bottles + non-PVC tubing (NTG absorbs into plastic)
Transdermal patch12 hours on / 12 hours off. Rotate sites. Apply to hairless, clean, dry skin

4.4 Storage

Dark glass bottle, tightly closed, replace every 6 months. Keep away from heat, light, and moisture.

4.5 Side Effects

  • Headache (#1 most common — vasodilation of cerebral vessels; shows drug is working)
  • Hypotension, dizziness, flushing
  • Reflex tachycardia
ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION: PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil/Viagra, tadalafil/Cialis) within 24-48 hours. Can cause fatal hypotension. ALWAYS ask about erectile dysfunction medications before giving NTG.
Hold NTG if: SBP <90 mmHg, PDE-5 inhibitor use, or suspected right ventricular infarction (these patients are preload-dependent — NTG can crash their BP).
Nitrate-free interval: 12h on / 12h off for patches prevents tolerance development.

5. MONA & ACS Medications HEAVY IN SLIDES

M O N A Morphine • Oxygen • Nitroglycerin • Aspirin

5.1 Classic MONA (Updated Thinking)

DrugCurrent Recommendation
MorphineUse cautiously — only if pain not controlled by NTG. Can increase mortality, cause hypotension/respiratory depression
OxygenOnly if SpO2 <94%. Routine O2 not recommended
NitroglycerinSublingual for chest pain (if no contraindications)
Aspirin162-325mg CHEW immediately — first drug given
VOMIT = what NOT to give routinely: Vasopressors, Oxygen (if SpO2 >94%), Morphine, IV fluids (if not hypotensive), Trendelenburg.

5.2 ACS Treatment Sequence

  1. Aspirin 162-325mg — CHEW immediately (speeds absorption by 50%)
  2. NTG sublingual — for chest pain relief
  3. Heparin (UFH or enoxaparin) — prevents clot extension
  4. Beta-blocker within 24h (metoprolol) — reduces mortality, limits infarct size
  5. ACE inhibitor within 24h — reduces remodeling
  6. High-intensity statin at discharge (atorvastatin 80mg) — regardless of baseline cholesterol

5.3 Reperfusion Timing

"Time is muscle" — every minute of delay = more myocardial death.
  • STEMI door-to-balloon: <90 minutes (PCI)
  • STEMI door-to-needle: <30 minutes (fibrinolytics, if PCI not available)

6. Antiarrhythmic Medications HEAVY IN SLIDES

6.1 Key Emergency Antiarrhythmics

DrugDoseIndicationKey Points
Atropine0.5mg IV q3-5min, max 3mgSymptomatic bradycardiaBlocks vagus nerve (parasympatholytic) → ↑ HR. NOT for Mobitz II or 3rd-degree block
Adenosine6mg rapid IV push, then 12mgSVTRapid push + 20mL NS flush; half-life <10 seconds. Warn patient about brief "heart stopping" feeling
Amiodarone300mg IV push (arrest), 150mg (2nd)VT/VF, A-fib"Amio does everything" — multiple ion channels. Long-term: pulmonary, thyroid, liver, eye toxicity
Magnesium sulfate1-2g IVTorsades de pointesDrug of choice for polymorphic VT with long QT
"Adenosine = reset button for SVT." Must use closest IV port with rapid push — half-life is <10 seconds!

6.2 A-fib Management

StrategyGoalDrugs
Rate controlControl how FAST ventricles beatMetoprolol, diltiazem, digoxin
Rhythm controlRestore NORMAL sinus rhythmAmiodarone, flecainide, sotalol
Amiodarone long-term side effects: Pulmonary fibrosis (PFTs), thyroid dysfunction (TFTs), hepatotoxicity (LFTs), corneal deposits (eye exam), photosensitivity, "blue-gray" skin discoloration. Requires baseline and ongoing monitoring.
Atropine won't work for infranodal blocks (Mobitz Type II, 3rd-degree with wide QRS) because it works on vagal tone at the SA/AV node level. These patients need transcutaneous pacing.

7.1 The 6 Lipid-Lowering Drug Classes

7.2 Targets

  • LDL goal: <100 mg/dL (general); <70 mg/dL (high risk / post-ACS)
  • HDL: higher is better (>40 men, >50 women)
  • Triglycerides: <150 mg/dL
HDL = "Happy" (want HIGH). LDL = "Lousy" (want LOW). Statins = Standard of care.
Statin nursing considerations: Take at bedtime (peak cholesterol synthesis at night — newer statins like atorvastatin can be taken anytime). Monitor LFTs. Report unexplained muscle pain immediately (check CK level for rhabdomyolysis).

8. Hypertension Medications HEAVY IN SLIDES

8.1 First-Line Drug Classes

ACE/ARBs, CCBs, thiazide diuretics (ABCD mnemonic).

8.2 Special Populations

PopulationPreferred Drug(s)Rationale
African AmericanCCB or thiazideACE/ARBs less effective as monotherapy
DiabetesACE/ARBRenal protection (reduce proteinuria)
PregnancyLabetalol, nifedipine, methyldopaACE/ARBs are TERATOGENIC
ACE/ARBs in pregnancy = TERATOGENIC. Never prescribe to pregnant patients.

8.3 Hypertensive Crisis

TypeDefinitionTreatmentBP Goal
EmergencyBP >180/120 WITH organ damageIV drugs: nicardipine, clevidipine, labetalol IV, nitroprussideReduce MAP by no more than 25% in first hour
UrgencyBP >180/120 WITHOUT organ damageOral agents: captopril, clonidine, labetalol POReduce over 24-48 hours
Never drop BP too fast — risk of watershed infarction (stroke), MI, and acute kidney injury from sudden hypoperfusion. Gradual reduction: 25% in first hour, normalize over 24-48h.

8.4 Additional Drug Notes

  • Thiazide diuretics: Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hyperglycemia, hyperuricemia, hypercalcemia
  • Clonidine: Central alpha-2 agonist. Rebound hypertension if stopped abruptly
  • Resistant HTN: BP above goal despite 3+ drugs at max dose including a diuretic — rule out secondary causes

9.2 Non-Pharmacologic Management

  • Sodium restriction: <2g (2000mg) per day
  • DASH diet: Fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy
  • Daily weights: Same time, same scale, same clothing
  • Fluid restriction: If severe HF (typically 1.5-2L/day)
  • I&O monitoring
"Start low, go slow" with beta-blockers in HF. They improve long-term outcomes but can worsen acute symptoms. Never initiate during acute decompensation.

10. Emergency & Code Medications HEAVY IN SLIDES

10.1 Cardiac Arrest Drugs

DrugDoseIndicationKey Points
Epinephrine1mg IV/IO q3-5minALL arrest rhythms (VF, pVT, PEA, asystole)"Epi for everything in a code"
Amiodarone300mg (1st), 150mg (2nd)VF/pVT refractory to defibrillationGiven AFTER 3rd shock + epi
Sodium bicarbonate1 mEq/kgHyperkalemia, TCA OD, prolonged arrest with acidosisNOT routine in all arrests
Magnesium sulfate1-2g IVTorsades de pointesAlso treat hypomagnesemia
Calcium chloride/gluconatePer protocolHyperkalemia, CCB overdoseCaCl is 3x more potent than gluconate
"Epi for everything in a code" — 1mg IV/IO q3-5min regardless of rhythm. It's the universal cardiac arrest drug.

10.2 Bradycardia Drugs

  • Atropine: 0.5mg IV q3-5min, max 3mg (for symptomatic bradycardia only — NOT for arrest)
  • Dopamine: 5-20 mcg/kg/min (if atropine fails)
  • Epinephrine infusion: 2-10 mcg/min (alternative to dopamine)

10.3 Vasopressors in Emergencies

  • Norepinephrine: First-line for septic shock
  • Dopamine: Alternative to norepinephrine
  • Vasopressin: Adjunct pressor (non-catecholamine)

10.4 Thrombolytics

DrugUseTiming
Alteplase (tPA)STEMI, ischemic stroke, massive PEStroke: door-to-needle <60 min (within 3-4.5h window)
TenecteplaseSTEMI (preferred — single IV bolus)STEMI: door-to-needle <30 min
Thrombolytic contraindications: Active bleeding, recent surgery, hemorrhagic stroke, uncontrolled HTN, known bleeding disorders. These drugs DISSOLVE clots — bleeding risk is the #1 concern.

10.5 Epinephrine for Anaphylaxis

0.3-0.5mg IM (1:1000) into lateral thigh. Repeat q5-15 min if needed. Note: different dose/route than cardiac arrest (which is 1mg IV of 1:10,000).

Know your H's and T's — treat the CAUSE, not just push drugs.
  • H's: Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, Hydrogen ion (acidosis), Hypo/Hyperkalemia, Hypothermia
  • T's: Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade, Toxins, Thrombosis (PE/MI)

Practice Questions

NCLEX-style questions covering key medications across all sections.

1. A patient with chest pain has taken 3 sublingual NTG tablets without relief. The nurse should:

Answer: Activate emergency response / call 911. After 3 doses at 5-minute intervals with no relief, this suggests possible MI requiring emergent intervention (PCI or fibrinolytics).

2. Before giving NTG, what medication history must the nurse assess?

Answer: PDE-5 inhibitor use (sildenafil/Viagra, tadalafil/Cialis) within 24-48 hours. Concurrent use can cause severe, potentially fatal hypotension.

3. A patient on heparin has an aPTT of 110 seconds. The nurse should:

Answer: HOLD the heparin infusion and notify the provider. aPTT >100s indicates excessive anticoagulation. Recheck aPTT after holding. Antidote: protamine sulfate if active bleeding.

4. A patient on digoxin has a heart rate of 54 bpm. The nurse should:

Answer: HOLD the digoxin and notify the provider. Hold parameter is HR <60. Check dig level (therapeutic: 0.5-2.0 ng/mL) and potassium level (hypokalemia increases toxicity).

5. Which lab value increases the risk of digoxin toxicity?

Answer: Hypokalemia (low K+). Digoxin and potassium compete for the same binding sites. When K+ is low, more digoxin binds → increased toxicity risk.

6. An African American patient with HTN. Which first-line drug is preferred?

Answer: Calcium channel blocker (amlodipine) or thiazide diuretic. ACE/ARBs are less effective as monotherapy in this population. Can still be added as combination therapy.

7. During a code, the patient is in VF. After the 3rd shock and epinephrine, which drug is given?

Answer: Amiodarone 300mg IV push. For refractory VF/pVT after defibrillation. Second dose is 150mg.

8. A patient asks why their statin should be taken at bedtime. The nurse explains:

Answer: Cholesterol synthesis peaks at night, so taking the statin at bedtime maximizes its effectiveness. Note: Newer statins like atorvastatin and rosuvastatin have longer half-lives and can be taken at any time.

9. A patient on warfarin has an INR of 5.2 with no signs of bleeding. The nurse anticipates:

Answer: Hold warfarin and administer low-dose vitamin K (1-2.5mg PO). Recheck INR. If INR >9 without bleeding, higher-dose vitamin K may be needed. For active bleeding: FFP or PCC for rapid reversal.

10. A patient in heart failure gains 4 pounds overnight. Priority nursing action?

Answer: Notify the provider immediately. Weight gain >2 lbs overnight suggests fluid retention/worsening HF. Expect orders for increased diuretic dose. Assess for edema, lung sounds, dyspnea.

11. Adenosine must be given via rapid IV push because:

Answer: Its half-life is less than 10 seconds. If given slowly, the drug is metabolized before reaching the heart. Must use proximal IV site and follow immediately with 20mL NS flush.

12. A patient is prescribed an ACE inhibitor and develops a persistent dry cough. The nurse expects:

Answer: Switch to an ARB. Dry cough is a common ACE side effect (caused by bradykinin accumulation). ARBs have similar benefits without the cough.

13. Which HF medication does NOT reduce mortality?

Answer: Digoxin. Digoxin improves symptoms and reduces hospitalizations but does NOT reduce mortality. Drugs that DO reduce mortality: ACE/ARBs, beta-blockers, aldosterone antagonists, ARNI (Entresto), SGLT2 inhibitors.

14. Atropine is ineffective for which type of heart block?

Answer: Infranodal blocks (Mobitz Type II, third-degree with wide QRS). Atropine works by blocking vagal tone at the SA/AV node. Infranodal blocks are below the AV node, so atropine won't help. These patients need transcutaneous pacing.

15. A patient in hypertensive emergency (BP 220/130). How quickly should BP be lowered?

Answer: Reduce MAP by no more than 25% in the first hour. Dropping BP too fast risks watershed infarction (stroke), MI, and acute kidney injury. Then normalize gradually over 24-48 hours.
