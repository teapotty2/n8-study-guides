EXAM TRAP: 3rd-degree burns are INSENSATE — no pain is a finding, not reassurance. Never tell the patient "it's not that bad because it doesn't hurt." That means full-thickness nerve destruction.

TBSA Estimation

Rule of Nines

  • Head: 9%
  • Each arm: 9%
  • Anterior trunk: 18%
  • Posterior trunk: 18%
  • Each leg: 18%
  • Perineum: 1%

Lund & Browder

  • Most precise
  • Adjusts head/leg % for age
  • Reassess within 72h

Palmar Method

  • Patient's own hand (with fingers) = 1% TBSA
  • Best for scattered/irregular burns

Fluid Resuscitation

Formula

Thermal/Chemical: 2 mL × kg × %TBSA

Electrical: 4 mL × kg × %TBSA

Fluid: Lactated Ringer's ONLY (closest pH/osmolality to plasma)

Timing: ½ in first 8h, ½ over next 16h

Urine Output Targets (Resuscitation Adequacy)

Thermal/Chemical: 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr

Electrical: 75–100 mL/hr (until urine clears — red urine = myoglobinuria)

UO is the gold standard for resuscitation adequacy. BP and HR alone are NOT reliable in burns.

EXAM TRAP #1: The clock starts at time of injury — NOT ED arrival. If burned at 10 AM and arrives at noon, you are already 2 hours into the first 8-hour window.
NEVER in Burns:
  • NEVER apply ice (worsens damage, hypothermia)
  • NEVER use NS as primary fluid (causes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis)
  • NEVER add KCl to burn IVF (hyperkalemia already present from cell lysis)
  • NEVER use wet-to-dry dressings (tears new granulation tissue)

ABA Burn Center Referral Criteria

  • Partial thickness ≥10% TBSA
  • Burns of face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum, major joints
  • Third-degree burns (any size)
  • Electrical or lightning burns
  • Chemical burns, inhalation injury
  • Preexisting medical disorders
  • Concomitant trauma
  • Children in non-pediatric facilities
  • Special social/emotional/rehab needs

Skin Grafting

Autograft (Gold Standard)

  • Patient's own skin; no rejection
  • Split-thickness; sheets or meshed
  • First dressing change: 3–5 days post-op
  • Graft loss: infection, bleeding under graft, shearing

Homograft (Allograft)

  • Cadaver skin; temporary
  • WILL be rejected
  • Purulent exudate → notify provider
  • Do NOT independently debride

Xenograft (Pigskin)

  • Temporary
  • Separating = trim away — EXPECTED, not an emergency

Burn Systemic Effects (Burns >20% TBSA)

Immediate Effects

  • Cardiovascular: Immediate ↓CO → burn shock (hypovolemic); peak capillary leak 6-8h; ↑Hct (hemoconcentration)
  • Electrolytes: Hyperkalemia (cell lysis) + Hyponatremia (Na+ lost in edema)
  • Pulmonary: Upper airway edema takes UP TO 48H to develop (not immediate!)
  • CO poisoning: SpO2 is UNRELIABLE; treat with 100% O2 until carboxyhemoglobin <5%

Complications to Know

  • Curling's ulcer: Gastric/duodenal erosion from ischemia (stress ulcer); "coffee-ground" emesis
  • Paralytic ileus: >20-25% TBSA → NG decompression
  • Compartment syndrome: Circumferential burns → escharotomy/fasciotomy
  • AKI: Hypovolemia + myoglobin in tubules → red urine
  • Zones: Coagulation (irreversible) → Stasis (nursing saves this zone) → Hyperemia (spontaneous recovery)

Airway Management — Escalation Hierarchy

  1. Head-tilt/chin-lift; jaw-thrust (C-spine protection)
  2. OPA — unconscious, spontaneous breathing; measure lip to ear
  3. NPA — NOT with facial trauma or basal skull fracture
  4. Endotracheal intubation (ETT)
  5. King Tube / LMA — field airway
  6. Cricothyroidotomy — maxillofacial trauma, C-spine injury, laryngeal edema

Cyanosis and LOC = LATE signs — do not wait for these to intervene.

Key ED Situations

Hemorrhage Management

  • Two large-bore IVs (uninjured extremity if possible)
  • LR preferred (NS causes hyperchloremic acidosis)
  • Warm blood products (cold → cardiac arrest/coagulopathy)
  • Direct firm pressure; tourniquet when pressure fails
  • Document tourniquet: label "T" + time applied; leave until OR
  • Triad of death: Hypothermia + Acidosis + Coagulopathy

Special Situations

  • Evisceration: Sterile moist saline dressing; hold oral fluids; NG; antibiotics
  • Rhabdomyolysis: Myalgias + dark (cola) urine + CK >6000 IU/L (CK-MM isoform, not CK-MB); min UOP 30 mL/hr until urine clears; fasciotomy if compartment syndrome; abdominal compartment syndrome measured via Foley catheter device
  • Sexual assault: Paper bags (NOT plastic) for evidence; hang wet bloody clothing to dry, then paper bag; water-moistened speculum; cut AROUND bullet holes and stab wounds — NEVER through them; chain of custody
  • IO access: Intraosseous good for 24 hrs when no IV possible; O-negative uncrossmatched blood in emergencies; pressure bag for rapid transfusion
  • "Turn of death": Keep hemodynamically unstable patients supine — turning can drop BP and cause cardiac arrest
  • Alcohol withdrawal: DTs start 48-96h after last drink; CIWA protocol; private quiet room; NO TV (seizure risk)

Nerve Agents / Chemical

  • Nerve agents (Sarin, VX): SLUDGE — Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, GI, Emesis + bronchospasm
  • Treat: Atropine + pralidoxime + benzos; soap & water decon
  • Mustard (vesicant): Blistering; blot (do NOT rub); dimercaprol systemic
  • Cyanide (blood agent): Bright red skin → respiratory then cardiac arrest; hydroxocobalamin; soap and water decon
  • Radiation: Time + distance + shielding + dosimeter; Contamination = decontaminate; Incorporation = medical management; External irradiation = NOT contagious, not an emergency by itself

Sepsis 6-Hour Bundle

  1. Measure lactate (repeat if >2 mmol/L)
  2. Blood cultures ×2 before antibiotics
  3. Broad-spectrum antibiotics (within 1 hour of recognition)
  4. 30 mL/kg IV crystalloid (NS or LR) if hypotensive or lactate ≥4
  5. Vasopressors (NE) ONLY if still hypotensive after fluids
  6. Reassess fluid status and tissue perfusion continuously

Family Support & Death Care PROF HINTED DIRECTLY — READ THE SLIDE

Before Allowing Family to View Deceased

  • Clean the patient — remove or tuck ET tube, change bloody/soiled linens, wash visible blood
  • Clean the room — the last image the family has matters; do not bring family in immediately after a code if the room is in disarray
  • Position patient with dignity; cover appropriately
  • Assign a dedicated nurse to stay with the family

During a Code / Grief Communication

  • Family wanting to watch a code: Allow it — helps them understand that everything possible was done; reduces complicated grief
  • After death: sit down with family, use clear language (not "passed away" if unclear — be direct but compassionate)
  • Holistic principle: Patient is a full person — they have family, a life, a job; care does not stop at the clinical problem
  • Chaplain/social work referral; organ donation discussion when appropriate

Cardiac Output Fundamentals

The Master Formula

CO = HR × SV  |  Normal: 4–6 L/min

Preload (volume/stretch at end-diastole)Hemorrhage, dehydration → fill the tank (fluids)Fluid overload, HF → diuretics, fluid restrict
Afterload (resistance to ejection)Vasodilation (sepsis) → vasopressorsHTN, vasoconstriction → A-B-C-D meds

A-B-C-D = Afterload Reduction

  • A — ACE inhibitors & ARBs (lisinopril, losartan)
  • B — Beta-blockers (metoprolol, carvedilol)
  • C — Calcium channel blockers (diltiazem, verapamil)
  • D — Diuretics + vasodilation (furosemide, nitroglycerin)

Positive Inotropes

MONA — Initial ACS Management

Morphine (pain & anxiety; use cautiously — may mask symptoms) · O2 (if SpO2 <94%) · Nitroglycerin (hold if SBP <90 mmHg or right ventricular MI) · Aspirin (325mg chewed)

Plus: Heparin (anticoagulation) · Beta-blocker · ACE inhibitor (within 24h for STEMI)

Heart Failure

Left HF → Pulmonary Congestion (backward into lungs)

  • Dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, PND
  • Crackles (fine, basilar)
  • S3 gallop (ventricular volume overload)
  • Pink frothy sputum (acute pulmonary edema)
  • Fatigue, ↓exercise tolerance

Right HF → Systemic Congestion (backward into body)

  • JVD (jugular venous distension)
  • Dependent pitting edema (ankles, feet)
  • Hepatomegaly, ascites
  • Weight gain (fluid retention)
  • Most common cause = Left HF

HF Treatment

ACE inhibitors/ARBs (↓afterload) · Beta-blockers (↓HR, ↓remodeling) · Furosemide (↓preload/fluid) · Spironolactone · Digoxin (↑contractility + ↓HR; narrow TI; K+ affects toxicity)

Position: HOB elevated (high-Fowler's for acute); daily weight (report >2 lb/day gain)

Pacemaker Malfunction PROF SAID MOST STUDENTS MISSED THIS ON EXAM 1

Failure to Capture

Pacing spike fires but NO QRS follows — myocardium does not respond to the signal

ECG: Spike present, no depolarization after it

Causes: Battery depletion, dislodged/fractured lead, increased pacing threshold, fibrosis at lead tip

Fix: Increase milliamps (amplitude/output); check lead position; replace battery

Failure to Sense

Pacemaker does NOT detect the patient's own heartbeat → fires inappropriately (competes with intrinsic rhythm)

ECG: Pacing spikes appear where they shouldn't (on top of or near intrinsic beats) — can land on T wave → VF risk

Causes: Battery depletion, dislodged lead, electromagnetic interference, sensitivity set too low

Fix: Increase sensitivity; reposition lead; check for EMI sources

Memory: Capture = contraction. Sense = sensing. Failure to capture → spike but no beat. Failure to sense → pacemaker is "blind" to what the heart is already doing.

TTM | Hypertensive Crisis | Cardiomyopathy | Infectious Heart | CABG

Targeted Temperature Management (TTM)

  • What: Controlled cooling to 32–36°C (89.6–96.8°F) after cardiac arrest
  • Who (candidates): Adults with ROSC (return of spontaneous circulation) after cardiac arrest — especially VF/pulseless VT; remains comatose after ROSC
  • Duration: 24 hours, then controlled rewarming
  • Goal: Reduce neurologic injury from ischemia/reperfusion
  • Monitor temp continuously; prevent shivering (worsens outcome)

Hypertensive Crisis

  • Hypertensive urgency: Severe HTN, NO end-organ damage → oral meds, lower over hours/days
  • Hypertensive emergency: Severe HTN + end-organ damage (stroke, MI, renal failure, papilledema) → IV meds, ICU
  • Rule: Reduce MAP by no more than 25% in the first hour — too rapid → ischemia (brain, heart, kidneys)
  • IV agents: Nitroprusside, labetalol, nicardipine

CABG & Valvular Disease

  • CABG grafts: Greater saphenous vein (leg, most common) and lesser saphenous vein; also internal mammary artery (longer patency)
  • Most common post-CABG complication: Atrial fibrillation (peaks 2-3 days post-op)
  • Aortic stenosis: Most common valvular disease in elderly; systolic murmur at 2nd RICS radiating to carotid; angina, syncope, dyspnea = bad triad → surgery
  • Mitral regurgitation: Blood backflows into atria; LA/LV enlarge; systolic murmur at apex radiating to axilla
  • Valve replacement: Mechanical (lifelong warfarin) vs. Bioprosthetic (no long-term anticoagulation, less durable)

Nitroglycerin Patient Education PROF EMPHASIS

  • Carry NTG at all times (must be accessible immediately)
  • Sit or lie down before taking (causes hypotension → fall risk)
  • 1 tablet SL → wait 5 min → if pain persists, 2nd tablet → wait 5 min → if persists, 3rd tablet → call 911
  • Call 911 after 1st tablet if chest pain is new/severe (don't wait for 3 doses)
  • Oxygen reduces cardiac workload and decreases anginal pain
  • Store in dark glass bottle, away from heat/light; replace every 6 months
  • Hold if SBP <90 mmHg or if patient took PDE5 inhibitor (sildenafil) within 24-48h → severe hypotension

Increased ICP

Key Values & Formulas

Normal ICP: 0–15 mmHg

MAP = (SBP + 2×DBP) ÷ 3 — calculate this first, then CPP

CPP = MAP − ICP (normal 60–100 mmHg; <50 = irreversible damage; <60 = ischemia)

Monro-Kellie Hypothesis: Brain + Blood + CSF = constant. If one ↑, others must ↓ (or ICP rises)

Cushing's Triad = LATE / HERNIATION IMMINENT:

  • Hypertension (widened pulse pressure)
  • Bradycardia
  • Irregular respirations

ICP Nursing Interventions

  • HOB 30°, head/neck neutral (no rotation/flexion)
  • Avoid Valsalva (no straining, coughing — give stool softeners)
  • No clustering of care (rest between activities)
  • Quiet, dimly lit environment (minimize stimulation)
  • Avoid suctioning >10 seconds
  • CT scan FIRST before LP (rule out increased ICP → herniation risk)

Medical Management

  • Mannitol (osmotic diuretic; watch for dehydration)
  • Hypertonic saline (3% NaCl for herniation)
  • Hyperventilation (transient; ↓PaCO2 → vasoconstriction)
  • Dexamethasone (vasogenic edema from tumors)

Stroke

Ischemic Stroke (87%)

  • Thrombus or embolus; Afib is #1 embolic source
  • CT scan first (rule out hemorrhage)
  • tPA window: 3–4.5 hours from symptom onset
  • tPA contraindications: hemorrhagic stroke, recent surgery, anticoagulation, BP >185/110
  • During tPA: monitor BP q15min, no anticoagulants × 24h
  • Goal: BP <180/105 during/after tPA

Hemorrhagic Stroke (13%)

  • Rupture: aneurysm or AVM
  • tPA is CONTRAINDICATED
  • Subarachnoid (SAH): "worst headache of life"
  • Nimodipine (calcium channel blocker) for SAH → prevents vasospasm
  • Control BP; ICP management; possible surgical clipping/coiling

Seizures

Nursing During a Seizure

  • Protect airway (turn lateral); suction as needed
  • Do NOT restrain movements
  • Nothing in the mouth (no tongue blade, no fingers)
  • Time the seizure (duration matters)
  • HOB flat → lateral position post-ictal
  • Padded bed rails; quiet environment
  • Post-ictal: confusion, exhaustion, amnesia — normal

Status Epilepticus

  • Seizure >5 minutes OR no recovery between seizures
  • Emergency → benzodiazepines FIRST (lorazepam IV or diazepam)
  • If benzos fail: fosphenytoin or levetiracetam IV
  • If refractory: propofol or phenobarbital drip
  • Risk: prolonged hypoxia → brain injury

Seizure Types

  • Tonic-clonic (grand mal): LOC + tonic phase → clonic jerking
  • Absence (petit mal): Brief staring; no LOC; common in children
  • Simple partial: No LOC; focal; may spread (Jacksonian march)

Migraine | GBS | MS | MG

Migraine

  • Throbbing, unilateral headache; photophobia, phonophobia, N/V
  • Triggers: Chocolate, aged cheese, red wine, stress, hormonal changes, bright lights, sleep changes
  • Medication of choice: Triptans (sumatriptan) — serotonin agonists; cause cerebral vasoconstriction
  • Abortive: NSAIDs, triptans, ergotamines; dark/quiet room
  • Preventive: beta-blockers, valproate, topiramate, amitriptyline

Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)

  • Mechanism: Post-infectious autoimmune demyelination of peripheral nerves (PNS)
  • Classic pattern: Ascending symmetric weakness — starts in feet, moves up; areflexia
  • Life threat: Respiratory failure when reaches diaphragm — monitor forced vital capacity
  • Treatment: IVIG or plasmapheresis; supportive; NO steroids (worsen)
  • Preceding infection: Campylobacter jejuni most common; also flu-like illness
  • Sensation: pain + paresthesia early; then weakness dominates

Multiple Sclerosis (MS)

  • Mechanism: Autoimmune demyelination of CNS (brain + spinal cord)
  • Classic pattern: Relapsing-remitting; episodes separated in time and location
  • Symptoms: optic neuritis (vision loss), diplopia, fatigue, spasticity, bladder dysfunction, cognitive changes
  • Uhthoff's phenomenon: Symptoms worsen with heat (hot shower, fever) — avoid heat
  • Treatment: Interferon-beta, glatiramer acetate (Copaxone); steroids for acute flares; symptom management
  • MRI: periventricular white matter plaques (lesions)

Myasthenia Gravis (MG)

  • Mechanism: Autoantibodies block ACh receptors at NMJ → muscle weakness
  • Pattern: Weakness worsens with activity, improves with rest; ocular muscles first (ptosis, diplopia)
  • Life threat: Myasthenic crisis — respiratory failure; Cholinergic crisis (too much pyridostigmine) — also respiratory failure; distinguish with Tensilon test
  • Treatment: Pyridostigmine (Mestinon — anticholinesterase); IVIG; plasmapheresis for crisis; thymectomy
  • Sensation is INTACT — pure motor disorder; this distinguishes from GBS

Pulmonary Hypertension (Brief)

  • Elevated pulmonary arterial pressure (>25 mmHg at rest) → right ventricular strain → cor pulmonale (right HF from lung disease)
  • Causes: COPD, PE, sleep apnea, left HF, idiopathic
  • Signs: dyspnea on exertion, fatigue, syncope, right HF signs (JVD, peripheral edema, hepatomegaly)
  • ECG: right axis deviation, right ventricular hypertrophy
  • Treatment: supplemental O2, diuretics, pulmonary vasodilators (sildenafil, bosentan); treat underlying cause
  • Cor pulmonale = right-sided HF caused by pulmonary disease — not the same as left HF causing right HF

Key Respiratory Conditions

COPD

  • Emphysema: barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing, ↑TLC/RV (air trapping), pink puffer
  • Chronic bronchitis: productive cough >3mo × 2 years; blue bloater
  • Hypoxic drive: Chronic CO2 retainers desensitize central chemoreceptors → breathe on low O2
  • Target SpO2: 88–92% for COPD (high-flow O2 suppresses respiratory drive)
  • Use Venturi mask for precise FiO2

Pulmonary Embolism (PE)

  • Virchow's Triad: Stasis + Hypercoagulability + Endothelial injury
  • Signs: Sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, hypoxia, hemoptysis
  • Massive PE: Obstructive shock → right heart strain (S1Q3T3 on ECG)
  • D-dimer elevated; CT pulmonary angiography (gold standard)
  • Treatment: Anticoagulation (heparin → warfarin); tPA for massive PE; embolectomy

Tension Pneumothorax

  • Signs: Absent/decreased breath sounds (affected side), tracheal deviation AWAY from affected side, ↑HR, ↓BP, JVD, cyanosis
  • Obstructive shock mechanism (compressed SVC, ↓venous return)
  • Priority: Needle decompression — 2nd ICS, midclavicular line, immediately
  • Then chest tube insertion

ARDS

  • Acute onset; bilateral infiltrates on CXR; not cardiac in origin
  • P/F ratio <300 (PaO2/FiO2)
  • Profound refractory hypoxemia despite O2
  • Nursing: Prone positioning; low tidal volumes (6 mL/kg); PEEP; fluid restriction
  • Often follows: sepsis, trauma, pneumonia, burns, aspiration

Asthma

  • Reversible bronchoconstriction; expiratory wheezing; accessory muscle use
  • Acute attack: SABA (albuterol) first → ipratropium → corticosteroids (IV methylprednisolone)
  • Status asthmaticus: Life-threatening; IV Mg sulfate; may require intubation
  • Peak flow monitoring: <50% personal best = severe; <25% = life-threatening
  • Pursed-lip breathing prolongs expiration and prevents air trapping
  • Silent chest (no wheeze) in severe asthma = ominous — no air moving

Pneumonia

  • CAP (community-acquired) vs HAP (>48h post-admission) vs VAP (ventilator-associated)
  • Signs: fever, productive cough, crackles/consolidation, ↑WBC, infiltrate on CXR
  • Priority nursing: HOB 30-45°, oral hygiene, encourage deep breathing/coughing, adequate hydration
  • VAP prevention: ABCDEF bundle, oral care with chlorhexidine, subglottic suctioning
  • Aspiration pneumonia: right lower lobe most common (due to anatomy when upright); left when supine
  • High-risk: elderly, immunocompromised, post-op, immobile

DIC — Disseminated Intravascular Coagulopathy

What It Is

Systemic activation of coagulation → simultaneous clotting AND bleeding. Clotting factors are consumed faster than they're made → paradoxical hemorrhage despite initial thrombosis.

Triggers: Major burns, crush injury, sepsis, trauma, obstetric emergencies (abruptio placentae), transfusion reactions, malignancy

Signs & Treatment

  • Bleeding from IV sites, mucous membranes, surgical wounds + simultaneous thrombosis
  • Labs: ↑PT, ↑aPTT, ↓fibrinogen, ↓platelets, ↑D-dimer, ↑FDPs
  • Treat the underlying cause first
  • Replace clotting factors: FFP, cryoprecipitate (fibrinogen), platelets
  • Heparin controversial — used only in thrombosis-dominant DIC
Rule of 15 for Hypoglycemia: 15g fast-acting carbs → wait 15 min → recheck. If <70, repeat. Unconscious: D50 IV or glucagon IM. If beta-blockers are masking symptoms, the patient may skip adrenergic warning signs — go straight to altered LOC.
