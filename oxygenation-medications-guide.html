All medications from the Oxygenation & Gas Exchange unit, organized by drug class with nursing considerations, dosing, and professor emphasis points.

I. Bronchodilators

Bronchodilators relax smooth muscle in the airways to increase airflow. They are the first-line treatment for acute bronchospasm and ongoing airway management in asthma and COPD.

SABAs (Short-Acting Beta-2 Agonists) — Rescue Inhalers

EXAM TIP: SABA = Rescue HIGH YIELD

SABAs are for acute symptom relief. If a question says "the patient is having an acute asthma attack, which medication do you give FIRST?" — the answer is always a SABA (albuterol).

Generic Brand MOA Key Nursing Points
Albuterol ProAir, Ventolin, Proventil Stimulates β2 receptors → bronchial smooth muscle relaxation Most common rescue inhaler. Side effects: tremor, tachycardia, palpitations, hypokalemia. Onset 5-15 min, duration 4-6 hours.
Levalbuterol Xopenex R-isomer of albuterol (same mechanism) Fewer cardiac side effects than albuterol. Used when tachycardia is a concern.
Terbutaline Brethine β2 agonist Also used as a tocolytic (stops preterm labor via uterine smooth muscle relaxation).

LABAs (Long-Acting Beta-2 Agonists) — Controller Meds

Warning: LABA Safety Rule

NEVER use a LABA alone for asthma — must ALWAYS be combined with an anti-inflammatory agent (ICS). LABAs alone increase the risk of asthma-related death. They are NOT rescue inhalers.

Generic Brand Key Notes
Salmeterol Serevent 12-hour duration. Use WITH ICS for asthma. Can be used alone for COPD.
Formoterol Foradil, Perforomist 12-hour duration. Faster onset than salmeterol.
Indacaterol Arcapta 24-hour ultra-long acting. COPD only.
Olodaterol Striverdi 24-hour. COPD maintenance.
Arformoterol Brovana Nebulized LABA for COPD.

Anticholinergics (Muscarinic Antagonists)

Generic Brand Duration Key Notes
Ipratropium Atrovent Short-acting (4-6 hrs) Blocks acetylcholine → bronchodilation via different pathway than β2 agonists. Often combined with albuterol (DuoNeb/Combivent).
Tiotropium Spiriva Long-acting (24 hrs) First-line maintenance for COPD. Once-daily dosing. Side effects: dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation.
Umeclidinium Incruse Ellipta Long-acting COPD maintenance. Dry powder inhaler.
Pearl: Anticholinergic Side Effects

Remember: "Can't see, can't pee, can't spit, can't sh*t" — blurred vision, urinary retention, dry mouth, constipation. These are classic anticholinergic effects across ALL anticholinergic drugs.

Methylxanthines

Theophylline (Theo-Dur, Elixophyllin)

Phosphodiesterase inhibitor → smooth muscle relaxation + mild anti-inflammatory

Route: PO
Use: Chronic asthma/COPD (rarely used today due to narrow therapeutic window)
Therapeutic Level: 5-15 mcg/mL. Toxicity is common.
Toxicity Signs: Nausea, vomiting, restlessness, seizures, arrhythmias
Nursing: Monitor serum levels, avoid caffeine, many drug interactions

II. Corticosteroids

Corticosteroids are anti-inflammatory agents that reduce airway edema, mucus production, and bronchial hyperresponsiveness. They are the most effective long-term controller medication for asthma.

Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) — Controller Meds

Generic Brand Key Nursing Points
Beclomethasone QVAR Rinse mouth after each use to prevent oral candidiasis (thrush).
Use spacer with MDI for better delivery and reduced oral deposition.
If also using a SABA, use SABA first (opens airways), then ICS (deeper penetration).
NOT for acute rescue — takes days to weeks for full effect.
Budesonide Pulmicort
Fluticasone Flovent
EXAM TIP: ICS Mouth Care HIGH YIELD

If a question asks about a patient on an inhaled steroid and they have white patches in their mouth — this is oral candidiasis (thrush) from the steroid. Teaching point: rinse, gargle, and spit after every use.

Systemic Corticosteroids

Generic Route Use Key Considerations
Prednisone PO Asthma/COPD exacerbation, sarcoidosis Short-term for acute exacerbations. Long-term use → muscle weakness, osteoporosis, adrenal suppression, hyperglycemia, immunosuppression, moon face, buffalo hump.
NEVER abruptly stop — must taper to avoid adrenal crisis.
Sarcoidosis: ~1 year of steroids, taper after 12 months.
Steroids + tube feeds = check blood sugars q6h.
Methylprednisolone IV/PO Severe exacerbations, COVID (MATH+)
Dexamethasone IV/PO Severe inflammation, post-extubation

III. Combination Inhalers

Combination Brand Components Notes
ICS + LABA Advair Fluticasone + salmeterol Asthma/COPD maintenance. Rinse mouth after use.
ICS + LABA Symbicort Budesonide + formoterol Asthma/COPD. Has faster LABA onset (formoterol).
ICS + LABA Breo Ellipta Fluticasone furoate + vilanterol Once-daily dosing. Dry powder inhaler.

IV. Other Respiratory Medications

Leukotriene Modifiers

Montelukast (Singulair) / Zafirlukast (Accolate)

Block leukotriene receptors → reduce inflammation, bronchoconstriction, mucus production

Use: Asthma prevention (not rescue), exercise-induced bronchospasm, allergic rhinitis
Route: PO only (taken at night for asthma)
Nursing: Monitor for mood/behavior changes (montelukast has an FDA black box warning for neuropsychiatric effects).

Mast Cell Stabilizers

Cromolyn sodium (NasalCrom, Intal)

Stabilizes mast cells → prevents histamine/leukotriene release

Use: Prophylactic ONLY. Given BEFORE exercise or allergen exposure.
Nursing: NOT effective for acute attacks. Takes 2-4 weeks for full effect.

Mucolytics & Expectorants

Drug MOA Use Key Notes
Acetylcysteine (Mucomyst) Breaks disulfide bonds in mucus CF, thick secretions Also the antidote for acetaminophen overdose. Smells like rotten eggs (sulfur).
Dornase alfa (Pulmozyme) Breaks down DNA in sputum Cystic fibrosis Reduces sputum viscosity. Nebulized once daily.
Guaifenesin (Mucinex) Increases respiratory fluid → thins mucus Chest congestion Increase fluid intake to help thin secretions. OTC.

CF-Specific Medications

Pancrelipase (Creon, Zenpep) PROF EMPHASIS

Pancreatic enzyme replacement — lipase, protease, amylase

Use: CF patients with pancreatic insufficiency (malabsorption)
Timing: Take BEFORE meals (to aid digestion)
Nursing: Do NOT crush the enteric-coated capsules. High-calorie diet recommended (CF patients lose weight from malabsorption).

Magnesium Sulfate

Magnesium Sulfate PROF EMPHASIS

Promotes smooth muscle relaxation

Dose: 2g IV over 20 minutes
Use: Status asthmaticus (when SABAs + steroids fail)
Nursing: Monitor for hypotension, flushing, muscle weakness. Check reflexes (loss of DTRs = toxicity). Have calcium gluconate at bedside (antidote).

Antibiotics for Pneumonia

Type Common Agents Key Notes
Community-Acquired PNA Azithromycin, fluoroquinolones (levofloxacin), amoxicillin Get sputum culture BEFORE starting antibiotics. Increase fluids.
Hospital-Acquired / VAP Broad-spectrum: piperacillin-tazobactam, vancomycin, carbapenems VAP develops ≥48 hrs after intubation. VAP bundle is prevention.
GAS Pharyngitis Penicillin V potassium × 10 days Full course essential to prevent rheumatic fever and glomerulonephritis.

Other Important Meds

Drug Use Key Notes
Chlorhexidine 0.12% Oral care for VAP prevention Applied q12h as part of the VAP bundle. Oral care with toothbrush q2h.
Antitussives (dextromethorphan, codeine) Suppress cough Do NOT suppress a productive cough — patient needs to expectorate secretions first. Only use for dry, non-productive cough.
PPIs (pantoprazole) PUD prophylaxis in VAP bundle Stress ulcer prevention for ventilated patients.
H2 blockers (famotidine/Pepcid) PUD prophylaxis (alternative) Alternative to PPIs in VAP bundle.

V. ICU Sedation Agents PROF EMPHASIS

Sedation is critical for ventilated patients — it prevents patient-vent dyssynchrony, reduces anxiety, and facilitates procedures. Have reversal agent orders ready BEFORE administering sedation (Pyxis/PRN).

Drug Brand Class Key Features Nursing Considerations
Propofol Diprivan Sedative-hypnotic Rapid onset/offset. White milky emulsion (lipid-based). Easily titratable. Monitor triglycerides (lipid load). Change tubing q12h (infection risk). Can cause hypotension, bradycardia. Propofol infusion syndrome if prolonged high doses.
Fentanyl Sublimaze Opioid analgesic 100x more potent than morphine. IV drip or bolus. Fast onset. Respiratory depression risk. Reversed by naloxone. Monitor RR, SpO2, ETCO2. Chest wall rigidity at high doses.
Midazolam Versed Benzodiazepine Anxiolytic + amnestic. Short-acting benzo. Respiratory depression risk. Reversed by flumazenil. Paradoxical agitation possible. Accumulates with renal impairment.
Ketamine Ketalar Dissociative anesthetic Unique: provides analgesia + sedation without respiratory depression. Bronchodilator properties (helpful in asthma). May cause hallucinations, increased secretions, elevated ICP.
Dexmedetomidine Precedex Alpha-2 agonist Sedation WITHOUT significant respiratory depression. Patient remains arousable. Ideal for weaning/extubation. Bradycardia and hypotension are main concerns. Does not require reversal agent. More expensive.
Warning: Both Opioids AND Benzos Suppress Breathing PROF EMPHASIS

Both opioids (fentanyl) and benzodiazepines (midazolam) suppress respiratory drive. When used together, the risk is compounded. Monitor respiratory rate, depth, SpO2, and ETCO2 continuously. Never leave a recently sedated patient unmonitored.

VI. Reversal Agents — MUST KNOW PROF EMPHASIS

Naloxone (Narcan)

  • Reverses: Opioids (fentanyl, morphine, heroin)
  • Dose: 0.2–0.4 mg IV q2-3 min PRN
  • Route: IV push; repeat until awake/breathing
  • Onset: 1-2 minutes IV
  • Duration: 20-60 minutes

Flumazenil (Romazicon)

  • Reverses: Benzodiazepines (Ativan, Xanax, Valium, Versed)
  • Dose: 0.2 mg IV; max 1 mg/dose, 3 mg total
  • Route: IV; give q few min, monitor closely
  • Onset: 1-2 minutes IV
  • Duration: 45-90 minutes
⚠️ Critical: Re-Sedation Risk PROF EMPHASIS

Both reversal agents have a SHORTER duration than the drugs they reverse. The patient CAN re-sedate after the reversal agent wears off. Continuous monitoring is essential — do not assume the patient is safe because they woke up.

VII. Anticoagulants (PE/DVT)

Drug Brand Route Monitoring Key Notes
Heparin (unfractionated) — IV drip (GTT) or SubQ aPTT, anti-Xa First-line for active PE. Rapid onset. Antidote: protamine sulfate.
Enoxaparin Lovenox SubQ Anti-Xa (if monitoring needed) LMWH — used for DVT/PE prevention and treatment. Do NOT rub injection site. Rotate abdominal injection sites.
Warfarin Coumadin PO INR (goal 2-3) Long-term anticoagulation. Takes 3-5 days for full effect (bridge with heparin). Antidote: Vitamin K (phytonadione). Avoid foods high in Vitamin K (leafy greens).
Clinical Pearl: DVT/PE Prevention in ICU

All ICU patients need DVT prophylaxis: SCDs (sequential compression devices) AND/OR SubQ heparin or enoxaparin. COVID patients are especially high-risk (hypercoagulable state). Ambulation as soon as possible.

VIII. Pulmonary Hypertension Medications

Drug Class Examples MOA Key Notes
CCBs Nifedipine, diltiazem, amlodipine Vasodilate pulmonary arteries First-line if vasoreactivity test is positive. Monitor BP closely.
Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors Sildenafil (Viagra/Revatio), tadalafil (Adcirca) Relax pulmonary vasculature via NO pathway Same drug as for ED — different indication! Do NOT combine with nitrates (fatal hypotension).
Prostanoids Epoprostenol (Flolan), treprostinil Potent pulmonary vasodilators Epoprostenol: continuous IV, cannot be interrupted (rebound pulmonary HTN). Keep ice packs on medication.
Endothelin receptor antagonists Bosentan (Tracleer), ambrisentan Block vasoconstrictor endothelin Hepatotoxic — monitor LFTs monthly. Teratogenic — requires pregnancy testing.
Diuretics Furosemide (Lasix) Reduce fluid overload For right-sided HF symptoms. Monitor I&O, daily weights, K+ levels.
Digoxin Lanoxin Improve RV contractility Narrow therapeutic index (0.5-2.0 ng/mL). Toxicity: bradycardia, N/V, visual disturbances (yellow/green halos). Check K+ (hypokalemia increases toxicity).
EXAM TIP: Sildenafil for Pulmonary HTN HIGH YIELD

Sildenafil (Viagra) for pulmonary hypertension is a classic test question. Same drug, different indication. Absolutely do NOT combine with nitrates — causes severe, potentially fatal hypotension.

+ ADJUNCTS

Remdesivir (Veklury) is the only FDA-approved antiviral for hospitalized COVID patients. Monitor liver and kidney function.

Pearl: Steroids + Tube Feeds

When a COVID patient is on steroids AND tube feeds, check blood sugars q6h. Steroids cause hyperglycemia, and the tube feed provides a continuous glucose load.

X. Smoking Cessation Aids

Type Examples Key Notes
NRT (Nicotine Replacement) Gum, inhaler, patch, sublingual tablet, lozenge Provides controlled nicotine to reduce withdrawal. Patch: new site daily, remove before sleep if causing vivid dreams.
Bupropion SR (Wellbutrin/Zyban) — Antidepressant that reduces nicotine cravings. Start 1-2 weeks before quit date. Lowers seizure threshold — avoid in seizure history.
Varenicline (Chantix) — Partial nicotine receptor agonist. Most effective cessation medication. Monitor for mood/behavior changes, suicidal ideation.
Other Nortriptyline, clonidine Second-line agents. Clonidine: monitor for hypotension.

XI. Quick Reference — Drug to Condition

Condition First-Line / Key Drugs Remember
Acute asthma attack SABA (albuterol) → systemic corticosteroids Rescue first, then control inflammation
Status asthmaticus SABA + IV steroids + IV fluids + Mg sulfate 2g IV Do NOT give non-selective beta-blockers. Metoprolol/atenolol OK.
COPD maintenance LABA + LAMA (tiotropium). ICS if frequent exacerbations. Flu vaccine annually. Target SpO2 ~90%, not 100%.
COPD exacerbation SABA + anticholinergic + systemic steroids + antibiotics (if infection) Do NOT over-oxygenate COPD patients.
Pneumonia (bacterial) Antibiotics (culture first!) + antipyretics. Increase fluids. Pneumo vaccine: PCV13 + PPSV23 one year apart (≥65 or immunocompromised ≥16)
Cystic fibrosis Chest PT + dornase alfa + pancrelipase + high-calorie diet Pancreatic enzymes BEFORE meals. Do NOT crush.
Sarcoidosis Corticosteroids for ~1 year, taper after 12 months Stiff lungs from granulomas. PFTs + ABGs for workup.
PE (active) Heparin GTT → transition to warfarin (INR 2-3) Prevention: SCDs + SubQ heparin/enoxaparin. D-dimer to rule OUT.
Pulmonary HTN CCBs, sildenafil, prostanoids, diuretics, digoxin Do NOT combine sildenafil with nitrates.
COVID-19 (hospitalized) MATH+ protocol + remdesivir Steroids + tube feeds = check glucose q6h.
Opioid respiratory depression Naloxone (Narcan) Shorter duration than opioid — watch for re-sedation.
Benzo respiratory depression Flumazenil (Romazicon) Shorter duration than benzo — watch for re-sedation.
VAP prevention Chlorhexidine q12h + oral care q2h + PPI/H2 + DVT prophylaxis VAP bundle: HOB >30°, daily SAT + SBT
ICU sedation Propofol, fentanyl, midazolam, ketamine, dexmedetomidine Have reversal agents ordered BEFORE sedation. SAT daily.
⚠️ Beta-Blocker Rule for Asthma PROF EMPHASIS

Do NOT give non-selective beta-blockers to asthma patients (propranolol, nadolol, etc.) — they block β2 receptors in the lungs causing bronchospasm. Cardio-selective beta-blockers (metoprolol, atenolol) are OK because they primarily target β1 (heart), not β2 (lungs).

Final Pharmacy Pearl

Always remember the inhaler order: if a patient takes both a SABA and an ICS, use the SABA first (opens the airways), wait 1-5 minutes, then use the ICS (so the steroid can penetrate deeper into the now-open airways). And always rinse the mouth after ICS use.
