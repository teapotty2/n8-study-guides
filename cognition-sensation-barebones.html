1. Neuroanatomy Essentials

Brain Lobes

Lobe Functions Damage Causes
Frontal (largest) Personality, judgment, motor, concentration, abstract thinking, Broca's area Personality changes, poor judgment, motor deficits, expressive aphasia
Parietal Sensory processing, spatial orientation, body awareness Sensory loss, neglect syndrome, inability to recognize objects by touch
Temporal Hearing, memory, Wernicke's area (language comprehension) Hearing deficits, receptive aphasia, memory problems
Occipital Vision processing Visual field deficits, blindness

Key Structures

  • Brainstem: Vital reflex centers — respiration, HR, BP, coughing, swallowing, vomiting. Contains reticular formation (arousal/sleep-wake)
  • Cerebellum: Coordination, balance, proprioception. Damage → ataxia
  • Hypothalamus: Temperature regulation, thirst, hunger, circadian rhythm
  • Motor fibers decussate at medulla → right brain stroke = left-sided deficits

Meninges & CSF

  • Three layers: Dura mater → Arachnoid → Pia mater
  • CSF circulates in the subarachnoid space
  • CSF produced by choroid plexus in ventricles, ~150 mL total, ~500 mL/day produced
  • Normal CSF: clear and colorless. Cloudy = infection. Pink/bloody = hemorrhage

Blood Supply

  • Internal carotid arteries (anterior, ~80%) + Vertebral arteries (posterior) → joined by Circle of Willis
  • Cerebral vessels have only 2 layers (not 3) → prone to aneurysm, especially at Circle of Willis
  • Cerebral veins have NO valves → depend on gravity for drainage → why HOB elevation matters
  • Blood-brain barrier disrupted by trauma, edema, hypoxemia

Monro-Kellie Hypothesis

Skull = rigid container. Three components: Brain (~1400g) + Blood (~75 mL) + CSF (~75 mL). If one increases, another must decrease → or ICP rises.

2. Neurotransmitters

NT Type Function Disease Link
Acetylcholine (ACh) Excitatory (muscle) / Inhibitory (cardiac) Muscle contraction, memory, parasympathetic effects ↓ in Myasthenia Gravis (antibodies attack ACh receptors) & Alzheimer's
Dopamine Both Motor control, reward, motivation ↓ in Parkinson's (substantia nigra); ↑ in Schizophrenia
Serotonin Inhibitory Mood, sleep, appetite ↓ in Depression; SSRIs increase it
Norepinephrine Excitatory Fight-or-flight, alertness, vasoconstriction Primary sympathetic NT; levophed = IV norepinephrine
GABA Primary INHIBITORY Reduces neuronal excitability ↓ = seizures; benzos & barbiturates enhance GABA
Glutamate Primary EXCITATORY Learning, memory Excess = excitotoxicity (secondary brain injury after stroke/TBI)
Endorphins Inhibitory Natural pain relief Opioids mimic endorphins
Demyelination (MS, GBS, SCI) → nerve impulses slow or stop. MS = CNS demyelination. GBS = peripheral. SCI transection = complete loss below level.

3. Autonomic Nervous System

Sympathetic (Fight or Flight)

NT: Norepinephrine + Epinephrine

  • ↑ HR, ↑ BP
  • Bronchodilation
  • Pupil dilation (mydriasis)
  • ↑ Blood glucose
  • ↓ GI motility
  • Blood → skeletal muscles
  • Diaphoresis

Parasympathetic (Rest & Digest)

NT: Acetylcholine

  • ↓ HR (bradycardia)
  • ↓ BP
  • Bronchoconstriction
  • Pupil constriction (miosis)
  • ↑ GI motility/secretions
  • ↑ Salivation
  • ↑ Urination
Connections: Neurogenic shock = lost sympathetic tone → give levophed. Vagal stimulation (CN X) = parasympathetic → bradycardia → give atropine. Autonomic dysreflexia = exaggerated sympathetic response below SCI lesion.

4. Neuro Assessment

LOC is the MOST SENSITIVE indicator of neurological status. Always the first thing to change.

Key Components

  • AAOx4: Person, Place, Time, Situation
  • Pupils: PERRLA — equal, round, reactive. Unilateral fixed dilated = CN III compression = emergency
  • Motor: Strength, coordination, gait
  • Sensory: Light touch, pain, temperature, proprioception
  • Cranial Nerves: I–XII (see cranial nerve flashcard set)
  • Reflexes: DTRs, Babinski (positive in adults = UMN lesion), corneal, gag

Reflexes

Increased DTRs (hyperreflexia) Decreased DTRs (hyporeflexia)
Upper motor neuron lesion, spinal cord compression, eclampsia, autonomic dysreflexia Lower motor neuron lesion, peripheral neuropathy, spinal shock, normal aging

LOC Progression

Alert → Confused → Lethargic (drowsy but arousable) → Obtunded (difficult to arouse) → Stupor (painful stimuli only) → Coma (unresponsive)

Breathing Patterns

  • Cheyne-Stokes: Crescendo-decrescendo with apnea — HF, brainstem damage
  • Kussmaul: Deep and rapid — DKA (blowing off CO₂)
  • Ataxic (Biot's): Completely irregular — brainstem damage (grave sign)

5. Diagnostic Tests

Test Purpose Key Points
CT scan First-line for stroke, trauma Within 20 min of ED arrival for stroke. Differentiates ischemic vs hemorrhagic
MRI Soft tissue detail, demyelination, DAI Remove ALL metal. Contraindicated with pacemakers
EEG Seizures, brain death Avoid caffeine before. Flat line = brain death
Lumbar Puncture CSF analysis (meningitis, SAH) CONTRAINDICATED with ↑ICP. Position: fetal. Post: ↑fluids for headache
Cerebral Angiography Aneurysm, AVM visualization Post: check puncture site, pedal pulses, neuro status

Lumbar Puncture Post-Care

  • Post-LP headache = CSF leakage → worsens upright, improves flat
  • Increase fluids, pain meds, position for comfort
  • Monitor neuro status and puncture site

6. Altered LOC & GCS

Glasgow Coma Scale (3-15)

Component Best Key Thresholds
Eye Opening (1-4) Spontaneous = 4 To verbal = 3, To pain = 2, None = 1
Verbal (1-5) Oriented = 5 Confused = 4, Inappropriate = 3, Incomprehensible = 2, None = 1
Motor (1-6) Obeys commands = 6 Localizes = 5, Withdraws = 4, Decorticate = 3, Decerebrate = 2, None = 1
GCS ≤ 8 = intubate. Patient cannot protect airway.

Posturing

  • Decorticate (flexion): Arms flexed toward core, legs extended. GCS motor = 3. "Cord still works"
  • Decerebrate (extension): ALL extended. GCS motor = 2. Brainstem damage. WORSE
  • Flaccid: No motor response. GCS motor = 1. Worst prognosis

Nursing Care for Altered LOC

  • #1 Priority: Airway. HOB 30°, lateral position, suctioning
  • Aspiration prevention — nothing by mouth until gag assessed
  • Skin: turning q2h, foam boots, correct alignment
  • Thermoregulation: Fever ↑ ICP. Treat aggressively but prevent shivering
  • Eyes: artificial tears, patches — can't blink (check corneal reflex)
  • Bowel: stool softeners, bowel regimen. Bladder: may need Foley
  • Talk to patient as if they can hear — hearing is last sense to go
  • Reduce stimulation. Space nursing activities (clustering ↑ ICP)

7. Increased ICP

Normal ICP: 0-10 mmHg (upper limit 15). Intervene when >15.

CPP = MAP − ICP   |   Target: 70-80 mmHg   |   CPP < 50 = irreversible damage

Signs (Early → Late)

  • EARLY: ↓ LOC (restlessness, confusion, delayed responses) — FIRST sign
  • MID: Pupil changes (ipsilateral dilation), headache, vomiting (projectile, no nausea)
  • LATE — Cushing's Triad: ↑ systolic BP + widened pulse pressure, bradycardia, irregular respirations → imminent herniation

CO₂ and Cerebral Vessels

  • ↑ CO₂ → vasodilation → ↑ ICP (bad)
  • ↓ CO₂ → vasoconstriction → ↓ ICP (but too much = ischemia; keep PaCO₂ ≥ 30)
  • Always monitor: are they breathing off their CO₂?

Management

  • HOB 30-45°, head midline, avoid neck flexion/rotation
  • Avoid Valsalva — use stool softeners
  • Mannitol (osmotic diuretic) — pulls fluid from brain
  • Corticosteroids (dexamethasone) — ONLY for brain tumor edema, NOT trauma
  • Do NOT bolus hypertonic saline — rapid sodium correction → osmotic demyelination
  • Hyperventilation only in emergent situations
  • Space nursing activities

Hyponatremia vs Hypernatremia

Hyponatremia (Na⁺ < 135)

Water shifts INTO brain cells → cerebral edema → confusion, headache, seizures, coma (Na < 120)

Hypernatremia (Na⁺ > 145)

Water shifts OUT of brain cells → cellular dehydration → restlessness, irritability, seizures, coma (Na > 160)

SIADH vs Diabetes Insipidus

SIADH Diabetes Insipidus
ADH ↑ (too much) ↓ (too little)
Urine ↓ output, concentrated ↑↑ output (>200 mL/hr), dilute
Sodium ↓ (dilutional hyponatremia) ↑ (hypernatremia)
Treatment Fluid restriction (<800 mL/day) Fluid replacement + desmopressin (DDAVP)

8. Intracranial Surgery

  • Craniotomy: Bone flap created, repositioned after. Craniectomy: Bone removed for decompression
  • Transsphenoidal: For pituitary tumors, through nasal cavity. Post-op: NO nose blowing, watch for DI and CSF leak
  • Cerebral edema peaks 24-36 hours post-op
  • Give preop education: what to expect, shaving, drains, frequent neuro checks
  • Post-op: neuro checks q15-60 min, seizure precautions, anticonvulsants (Keppra, phenytoin)
  • Signs of trouble: ↑ BP + ↓ HR + respiratory failure = increasing ICP
  • Blood-stained dressings → medium for bacteria → change them
  • No coughing, sneezing, nose blowing (↑ ICP)

9. Seizures

Abnormal uncontrolled electrical discharge. Any brain trauma = ↑ seizure risk.

Types

  • Focal (partial): One hemisphere
  • Generalized: Both hemispheres from onset
  • Status epilepticus: Continuous ≥5 min or serial without recovery → EMERGENCY → permanent brain damage

Nursing During Seizure

  • Stay with patient, protect from injury, clear area
  • DO NOT restrain. DO NOT put anything in mouth.
  • Turn to side, TIME the seizure
  • Check blood glucose immediately (brain not storing glucose)
  • Watch respiratory status — always

Medications

Drug Use Key Points
Lorazepam (Ativan) First-line active seizure 2 mg IV, repeat ×1. Also CIWA protocol
Diazepam (Valium) Alternative active seizure Longer half-life. Also CIWA
Levetiracetam (Keppra) Maintenance/prevention Given frequently in neuro; fewer drug interactions
Phenobarbital Prevention; status epilepticus Enhances GABA; respiratory depression risk
Phenytoin (Dilantin) Maintenance Therapeutic: 10-20 mcg/mL. Gingival hyperplasia

CIWA Protocol

Sliding scale lorazepam for alcohol withdrawal prevention. Goal: prevent progression to withdrawal seizures and delirium tremens.

10. Stroke

BeFAST: Balance, Eyes, Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911

Ischemic (87%) vs Hemorrhagic (13%)

Ischemic

  • Thrombus or embolus
  • Penumbra = salvageable tissue
  • tPA within 3 hrs (extended 4.5)
  • "Time is brain" — 1.9M neurons/min lost
  • A-fib = #1 embolic cause

Hemorrhagic

  • Ruptured vessel, AVM, anticoagulants
  • First sign: severe headache
  • tPA is CONTRAINDICATED
  • If SAH: nimodipine for vasospasm
  • Aneurysm precautions: bed rest, no stimulation

Aphasia

Type Location Presentation Memory Trick
Expressive (Broca's) Left frontal Can't speak/write, CAN understand Broca's = Broken speech
Receptive (Wernicke's) Left temporal Can't understand, speaks fluent nonsense Wernicke's = "What?"
Global Both areas Can't speak, write, or understand Everything lost

tPA Protocol

  • Dose: 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg). 10% bolus over 1 min, rest over 1 hour
  • Risk: BLEEDING. Insert all lines BEFORE giving
  • No anticoagulants for 24 hours after
  • Monitoring: q15 min × 2 hrs → q30 min × 6 hrs → q1 hr × 16 hrs
  • Permissive hypertension: allow BP up to 185/105 to perfuse penumbra

Additional Points

  • Dual antiplatelet: Aspirin + Plavix for secondary prevention
  • NIH Stroke Scale assessment ongoing, especially after thrombolytics
  • Swallow assessment before ANY food/drink
  • Right brain stroke = left deficits. Left brain stroke = right deficits + language issues

11. Head Injury & TBI

Epidural vs Subdural

Epidural Subdural
Location Between skull & dura Between dura & brain
Bleeding Arterial (middle meningeal) Venous (bridging veins)
Presentation Brief LOC → LUCID INTERVAL → rapid deterioration Acute (24-48 hrs) or chronic (weeks-months)
Treatment EMERGENCY surgery Monitoring or surgical evacuation
CT shape Biconvex (lens) Crescent

Key Concepts

  • Primary injury: Direct trauma at moment of impact (contusion, laceration, hematoma)
  • Secondary injury: Evolves over hours/days (edema, ischemia, glutamate excitotoxicity) — PREVENTABLE
  • Coup-contrecoup: Damage at impact site AND opposite side
  • Basilar skull fracture signs: Battle Sign (mastoid ecchymosis), Raccoon Eyes (periorbital), Halo Sign (CSF leak)
  • Always assume cervical spine injury until ruled out
  • Benzodiazepines preferred for sedation (don't affect CBF or ICP)

Brain Death

  • Cardinal signs: coma, absence of ALL brainstem reflexes, apnea
  • Apnea test: Off vent with O₂ cannula. No breathing with CO₂ to 60 mmHg over 8-10 min
  • Two physicians must confirm. Brain death = legal death

12. Spinal Cord Injury

Most common levels: C5-C7, T12, L1. Paraplegia (lower) vs Tetraplegia (all four, cervical).

Spinal Shock vs Neurogenic Shock

Spinal Shock Neurogenic Shock
What Loss of ALL reflex activity below injury Loss of sympathetic tone (distributive shock)
Signs Flaccid paralysis, areflexia, paralytic ileus ↓BP, bradycardia, warm dry skin
Duration Days to weeks (temporary) Acute emergency
Key distinction Reflex problem Circulatory problem

Neurogenic Shock Treatment

  • Try IV fluids FIRST but don't overload
  • Levophed (norepinephrine) — first-line vasopressor
  • Maintain MAP ≥ 85 mmHg
  • Usually in injuries above T6
Corticosteroids for SCI: NOW CONTROVERSIAL. Limited benefit, significant side effects. Many facilities stopped routine use.

Autonomic Dysreflexia

  • Who: SCI above T6, AFTER spinal shock resolves
  • Trigger #1: Distended bladder (kinked catheter)
  • Signs: Severe HTN, pounding headache, bradycardia, flushing/diaphoresis above lesion
  • FIRST action: SIT UP (gravity lowers BP), then find and remove cause
  • Hydralazine IV if BP stays critical

Other SCI Priorities

  • Respiratory: High cervical (C3-C5) affects phrenic nerve → diaphragm → may need ventilator
  • Secondary injury: Won't know extent until swelling resolves. Transected = complete, irreversible
  • DVT prophylaxis within 72 hrs for ≥3 months
  • PROM at least 4× daily. Splints for footdrop
  • Bowel program + intermittent cath (long-term)

13. Meningitis & CNS Infections

Classic Symptoms

Headache, high fever, nuchal rigidity, photophobia, ↓ LOC

Signs of Meningeal Irritation

  • Kernig: Thigh flexed → can't extend leg
  • Brudzinski: Neck flexion → reflexive hip/knee flexion (more sensitive)

Treatment

  • Bacterial: High-dose IV antibiotics (penicillin G + ceftriaxone) + dexamethasone. URGENT
  • Viral: Supportive care. Usually self-limiting (summer/fall)
  • Diagnosed by lumbar puncture: ↑ WBC, ↑ protein, ↓ glucose in bacterial
  • Droplet precautions until pathogen identified + 24 hrs on antibiotics
  • Prophylaxis for close contacts within 24 hours (rifampin, cipro, or ceftriaxone)
  • Petechial rash = N. meningitidis → septicemia

Nursing

  • Frequent neuro assessment, monitor for ↑ ICP, pain and fever management
  • HOB 30°, head midline
  • CLABSI bundle & CAUTI bundle — neuro patients often have lines + Foley

14. Autoimmune & CN Disorders

Multiple Sclerosis

  • Progressive CNS demyelination, relapsing-remitting pattern
  • 90% have spasticity, mostly lower extremities
  • Heat worsens symptoms — maintain temperate environment

Myasthenia Gravis

  • Antibodies attack ACh receptors at NMJ → weakness worsens with use
  • Initial: ocular muscles (ptosis, diplopia)
  • Treatment: Mestinon (pyridostigmine) — cholinesterase inhibitor
  • Thymectomy in patients <60 can produce remission
  • Tensilon test: IV edrophonium → immediate improvement = positive for MG

Myasthenic vs Cholinergic Crisis

Myasthenic Crisis

Undermedicated or exacerbation

→ ↑ Mestinon, IVIG, plasmapheresis

Cholinergic Crisis

Overmedicated with anticholinesterase

→ STOP meds, give atropine

Both cause severe weakness + respiratory compromise. Treatment is OPPOSITE.

Guillain-Barré Syndrome

  • Autoimmune attack on peripheral nerve myelin, 60-70% follow viral illness
  • Ascending paralysis (starts in legs, moves up) → respiratory failure = #1 threat
  • Treatment: ICU monitoring, plasmapheresis or IVIG

Trigeminal Neuralgia (CN V)

  • Severe STABBING facial pain. Triggers: touching face, brushing teeth, cold air
  • Treatment: carbamazepine (Tegretol) — monitor CBC (aplastic anemia risk)
  • Soft foods on unaffected side, lukewarm water, avoid drafts

Bell's Palsy (CN VII)

  • Sudden unilateral facial paralysis — NOT a stroke (reassure patient)
  • Bell's = ENTIRE face (including forehead). Stroke = lower face only (forehead spared)
  • Treatment: corticosteroids within 72 hours, eye protection, facial exercises
  • Most recover 3-5 weeks

15. Gerontologic Considerations

Change Significance
Brain weight ↓ Ventricles enlarge → ↑ risk subdural hematoma
Cerebral blood flow ↓ Slower processing (NORMAL). Memory loss = NOT normal — investigate
Slower reflexes DTRs diminished — expected
↓ Thirst sensation Prone to dehydration → confusion, falls
↓ Temperature regulation Prone to hypo/hyperthermia
↓ Pain perception Careful with hot/cold packs — burns/frostbite risk

16. Key Formulas

CPP = MAP − ICP   |   Normal CPP: 70-80 mmHg   |   CPP < 50 = irreversible damage
MAP = (SBP + 2×DBP) ÷ 3
GCS ≤ 8 → Intubate   |   Normal ICP: 0-15 mmHg
tPA: 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg) | 10% bolus, 90% over 1 hour | Window: 3-4.5 hrs
Top Priority Concepts:
• LOC = most sensitive indicator, always assess first
• ↑CO₂ = vasodilation = ↑ICP | ↓CO₂ = vasoconstriction = ↓ICP
• HOB 30-45°, head midline for ANY neuro patient
• Seizure: airway, don't restrain, don't put anything in mouth, TIME it
• Epidural = lucid interval → crash. Subdural = gradual
• Neurogenic shock: warm + bradycardia. Hypovolemic: cold + tachycardia
• Autonomic dysreflexia: SIT UP, check bladder FIRST
• Bell's = whole face. Stroke = lower face only
• Myasthenic = ↑ meds. Cholinergic = STOP meds + atropine
• Always watch respiratory status no matter what
